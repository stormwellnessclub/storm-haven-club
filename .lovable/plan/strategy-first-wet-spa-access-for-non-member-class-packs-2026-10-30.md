# Strategy first: wet spa access for non-member class packs

Nothing gets built until you choose a direction below. This plan covers the research, the options and the decisions that are yours to make.

## What the research shows
- Other clubs sell facility access as its own day pass, or bundle one class with recovery as a first-visit experience. Daily Dose sells a $65 "Recovery Stack": sauna, cold plunge, red light and one class [1](https://www.dailydosewellness.com/daypass). Spyre sells class packs and day passes separately [2](https://spyrecenter.com/packages-guest-passes/). Hive sells thermal access as an add-on to a day pass [3](https://hivefitclub.com/pages/day-pass).
- People usually need 5–6 visits before they commit to a membership [4](https://www.marianatek.com/blog/run-a-smarter-studio-5-boutique-fitness-trends-you-need-to-know/). Retention climbs steadily through the first visits and stays high from the fifth, which is also the most common point to convert [5](https://studio-deck.com/guides/convert-intro-offers-to-members).
- What this means for you: the wet spa day is your strongest membership preview. Pure Reformer studios nearby can't offer it. When you give it matters more than whether you give it.

## Options to choose from

**A. Day pass at purchase.** Simple, and it lifts the value of the 10-pack right away. The downside is that many people use it on day one, before they're hooked.

**B. Day pass unlocked after the 5th class.** It lands at the point where people tend to decide on a membership. It also rewards coming back, and staff can open a membership conversation that day. It needs a little more explaining at purchase.

**C. A "Class + Wet Spa" day sold separately.** This is a premium single visit for new people, separate from packs. It works well as an ad offer, but it's a new product with a new price.

**D. B plus a membership credit.** Unlock the day after class 5, and if they join within 14 days, part of the pack value goes toward initiation. This is the strongest path to membership, but it's more to manage.

## Things to keep in mind
- Ads promote Reformer and aerobics/studio only until cycling and yoga launch.
- Wet spa capacity: limit when the day pass can be used, for example weekdays or off-peak, so members' busy times aren't crowded.
- Wording: the day pass is "included" and never "free". It's one day per pack, not per class.
- The waiver and the club's rules (grip socks, check-in) apply on the spa day.

## Decisions needed from you
1. Which option, A, B, C or D?
2. Any blackout times for the day pass?
3. Should front desk get a prompt to offer membership on the spa day?
